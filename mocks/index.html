The problem

A question to the doctor can take 43 minutes.

At its farthest, Mars is 21 and a half light-minutes from Earth. A crew cannot wait that long for advice, and for twelve days it gets none at all.

What changes

Earth still sees everything and still gives the advice. Only the conversation moves on board.

  1. 1

    Measure all the time

    Suit, cabin and body are measured around the clock: every second on a Mars walk, every hour and every day in the habitat. Routines such as the morning check and the reaction test take minutes.

    Sensors and short self-tests
  2. 2

    Warn on the spot

    Limits from NASA's standards and from clinical practice are built in. When a value crosses one, the Arm Unit or the habitat says so at once and names the next action. Nothing waits for Earth.

    Fixed rules on board, no AI
  3. 3

    Earth thinks it through

    The same data reaches Earth minutes later. Doctors, trainers and specialists study it from every angle, with the time and the computing power a crew lacks, and send back one detailed instruction for the next day.

    People on Earth, with large AI models
  4. 4

    Just ask

    Astronauts do not want to be managed minute by minute, and after a working day nobody reads pages of analysis. So they talk. The assistant on board answers in plain words, right away.

    A voice assistant running on board

Rules decide, the assistant explains. It never raises or clears an alarm, and it does not diagnose. It says where each statement comes from: a measured value, the knowledge base, or Earth.

How to read the numbers

REAL
Measured, or computed from astronomy: the light delay, Mars seasons, radiation and weather replayed from real instruments.
SIM
Stands in for a sensor the crew would have, such as heart rate or cabin CO₂. Generated to behave like the real thing.
MODEL
An estimate that no sensor measures directly, such as stamina left or bone density between scans.
  • Not a medical device. A preview with simulated data, built to show what such a system could do.
  • Every limit has a source. NASA's spaceflight standards, clinical practice or published research; where we had to infer one, it says so.
  • Nobody has lived at Mars gravity. Every body value on the surface is a model between two known ends, weightlessness and Earth.

From Mars to Earth · a vision, not built

On Earth, many people are far from a doctor too.

Crews on polar stations, ships and offshore sites. People who live alone. Patients between two doctor visits. Villages with few doctors. They have more health and sensor data than ever, and often nobody at hand to read it with them. The approach we built for Mars carries over, block by block.
