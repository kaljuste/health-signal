What a Mars mission does to a body

NASA lists 22 ways a long spaceflight can harm its crew. We sorted them the way a crew would: first stay alive, then stay able to work, then come home healthy.

The list and the ratings are NASA's (Human Research Roadmap). The three tiers, the plain-language texts and the link to our measured values are ours. Select a risk to see what happens, how it starts, what we measure for it and what the two apps do.

      How this page was made

      • The list is NASA's Human Research Roadmap: 21 active risks and one inactive concern. NASA's public risk page shows 17 of them under shorter names.
      • The ratings are NASA's own. Its Human System Risk Board scores each risk by likelihood × consequence and gives it a colour, once for the mission itself and once for long-term health. We show the row "Mars Planetary (730–1224 days)" exactly as published, with the month of approval. The radiation rating still uses NASA's earlier 3×4 scale and is due for an update.
      • The three tiers are ours. NASA does not sort the list this way. We placed each risk where it would hurt the mission first: within hours or days, by wearing the crew down over weeks and months, or mostly after the return.
      • The order inside a tier follows NASA's rating: red before yellow, then likelihood × consequence. Tiers 1 and 2 use the in-mission rating, tier 3 the long-term rating. Equal scores, and the place of radiation, are our judgement. Bone sits in tier 3 by our judgement too: NASA's in-mission rating for it is red, but the loss is silent and its cost lasts for years after the return.
      • The explanations are written by us from our knowledge base (72 measured indicators with sourced thresholds). "NASA's wording" is quoted from the Roadmap.
      • The apps are a preview with simulated data. They are not a medical device: built-in rules decide, the voice assistant only explains.

      Source: NASA Human Research Roadmap, humanresearchroadmap.nasa.gov/Risks. Ratings as NASA publishes them; NASA updates them when its risk board meets.
