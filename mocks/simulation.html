How the simulation was made

One program run turns real data and published results into a complete mission: every day from launch to landing, four crew members, every rule result, Earth's instruction for each day, and four Mars walks second by second. The same run always gives the same mission.

    Does it behave like the real thing?

    We compared the simulated crew with what has been measured on real crews. The simulated numbers are read from the database as you open this page.

    What is assumed

    • Mars gravity. No human has lived at 0.38 g. Bone, muscle and balance on Mars are placed between weightlessness and Earth, and marked as assumptions.
    • Shielding in a storm. How much of a solar storm reaches the crew in the cabin and in the shelter is our stated assumption, not a calculation.
    • The next solar cycle. We assume the Sun behaves as it did at the same point of its last cycle.
    • The size of some effects. How much an illness costs in sleep, appetite and readiness is ours. NASA's data gives how often it happens, not how it feels.
    • Not checked against flight data beyond the table above. This is a preview, not a medical device.
